Professional Summary

PhD student (Penn State EE) working on efficient and reliable machine learning: training algorithms that skip the memory-heavy backward pass, software-level repair for AI models corrupted by hardware faults, and retrieval-augmented generation. Hands-on experience building RAG pipelines (Sentence Transformers, FAISS, HuggingFace) and implementing LLM architectures; coursework in large language models.

Experience

  • 2024 - present

    State College, PA

    Graduate Research Assistant
    The Pennsylvania State University
    • Developed STAR (arXiv:2607.15415): a retraining-based repair method for AI models deployed on memory chips that develop permanent hardware faults, requiring no additional hardware or fault localization. Before deployment, a compact snapshot of the healthy model’s internal states is stored (20x-191x smaller than a full checkpoint); after faults appear, this snapshot guides retraining to recover accuracy without any knowledge of which memory cells are broken. Proved the augmented training dynamics retain fixed-point convergence guarantees under Lipschitz-continuity conditions. Achieves +59% accuracy recovery over standard retraining on CNN and MLP benchmarks.
    • Working on scaling Equilibrium Propagation (EP), a local learning rule that computes weight updates without a backward pass, to deeper network architectures and developing methods to improve its computational efficiency; also investigating its theoretical foundations, including gradient-equivalence to backpropagation in appropriate limits, with a neuroscience-inspired angle drawing on glial-cell-mediated regulation of synaptic plasticity.
    • Built PyTorch/CUDA training pipelines for fault-injection experiments: simulating permanent memory failures at rates up to 90%, convergence diagnostics, and sweeps across MLP and CNN architectures.
